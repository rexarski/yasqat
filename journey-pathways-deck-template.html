Gain against prefix: state once whether it is a difference or a ratio
Three numbers per pathway. Lift: how much more likely than the average new customer is someone on this pathway to reach [outcome]. Gain against prefix: when we extend a pathway A-B by one more behaviour C, does C add information, or is it riding on A and B? We compare the [outcome] rate of A-B-C with that of A-B alone; that tells us which step is worth nudging. And reach: how many customers actually walk this path. A high rate on a few hundred customers is a curiosity; the same rate on tens of thousands is a programme. Honesty note: these are associations. They tell us where to intervene; whether the nudge causes the outcome is a test, and I'll come back to that in the ask.

Four steps, every time. One, define the situation: which customers, which window. Two, turn the activity log into journeys, one state per period per customer. Three, score the pathways: find the ordered behaviours that recur, and for each compute how often it leads to the outcome, how much more than baseline, and how many customers take it. Four, group whole journeys by similarity, so instead of one average customer we get a handful of typical journeys with a representative for each, and score pathways inside each group. What this buys us is repeatability: the month-[6] and month-[12] slides are the same notebook with one parameter changed. Point it at [outcome 2] and you get the same kind of slide back in days.

The fair question: "Why not just use [incumbent tool]? It's already in [platform]."

[Incumbent tool] finds frequent patterns. It doesn't answer "given this situation, how much more likely, and what's around it."

What [incumbent tool] does well

[Method 1]: what co-occurs in a basket, with confidence and lift.

[Method 2]: which ordered patterns are frequent, at cluster scale.

Zero build cost; it ships with [platform].

Where it stops

[Method 1] ignores order. A-then-B and B-then-A are the same basket.

[Method 2] returns patterns and counts only. No lift, no confidence, no gain, no rules for sequences.

Nothing around the miner: no situations, no durations, no journey similarity, no grouping.

Where our toolkit is weaker

Runs on one node; today's results come from a sample of the [N] cohort, not all of it.

Our pattern miner is simple and exhaustive; [Method 2] is faster on large alphabets.

We own it, so we maintain it.

Different jobs, not competitors. If we ever outgrow one node, [Method 2] generates the candidate pathways and the toolkit still scores, conditions, and groups them.

The fair question, and I'd rather raise it than have it raised: [incumbent tool] is right there in [platform] and it does frequent-pattern mining. Its due: [method 1] is excellent for basket questions; [method 2] finds frequent ordered patterns and scales across a cluster, and at very large scale it is the right tool and ours is not. Where it stops: [method 1] throws away order, and order is our signal. [Method 2] keeps order but returns patterns with counts only: no lift, no confidence, no gain, none of the three numbers on every card you just saw. And nothing around the miner: no situations, durations, journey similarity, or grouping. Our side of the ledger: the toolkit runs on one machine; today's results come from a sample of [N]; our miner is simple and exhaustive; and we own the maintenance. So: different jobs.
